# SOTA and novelty plan for Reflex-1 (2026-10-21)

Scope: what beat human records in racing games and drones, what is new in 2026, what our
byte-exact snapshot-able sim buys that none of them had, and the recipe. Constraints from
`LOOP.md`: no human runs, ghosts or leaderboard data touch anything that drives; the WR
22.123 s is only the number to beat. Every number below is from the cited primary source
or from this repo (`bench/infer/RESULTS.md`, `research/ENGINE.md`, `ctrl/sim.mjs`).

## Verdict (12 lines)

1. Nobody who beat a human record did it with a VLM in the loop. Linesight: 160x120 grey CNN + IQN, 10 of 12 campaign WRs. Sophy/GT7: 64x64 CNN + QR-SAC. Swift/MonoRace: gate detector + small net, 8 ms policy inference (Swift) to 500 Hz motor commands (MonoRace). The visual model is tiny and fast everywhere.
2. All of them are model-free RL at 50 ms (Linesight) or 100 ms (Sophy) action periods, trained for days, with the "last 0.1%" found by keeping the best of thousands of evaluation runs. Linesight literally saves `best_runs/{map}_{time}.inputs` as the record.
3. None had a snapshot-able, faster-than-realtime, byte-exact simulator. TMInterface has save states, but Linesight only rewinds to the map start state; GT runs in real time on PS4s with no state restore. Planning from arbitrary states was never on the table for them.
4. We have it: 114k ticks/s per core, 14 cores, 0.6 ms snapshot, 0.4 ms restore, 1 ms action grid. That makes the line a search problem (TAS-style), not an RL problem. Learning is only needed for perception and for speed at runtime.
5. So the recommended architecture is search-first, learn-second: an exact-sim planner finds and hardens a line, a tiny policy/value net makes search cheap, and the visual System One model only has to answer "where is my car, is it off-line" at 30 to 120 Hz.
6. The single most important idea is **jitter-robust search-distilled System One**: plan under the measured 1 to 3 ms key jitter so the line has margin (the WR has 0/40 finishes at +/-1 ms, `loop/score.mjs`), then distill the planner's per-state corrections into Valen's typed answers (AlphaZero expert iteration with a VLM student).
7. Expected: sim line under 22.123 s with >= 90% finish under jitter in days of CPU (P ~0.7); live from pixels under 22.123 s P ~0.4; most likely failure is the perception-to-pose error budget at 86 ms, not the planner.
8. New in 2026 that matters: RL fine-tuning of VLAs with verifiable rewards is standard, latency-aware RL (ARLI, Real-Time EXPO-FT) solves exactly our stale-observation problem, Dreamer 4 trains inside a learned world model on one GPU, and Jev-lineage papers (Jeva, Narrow Decision Models, Visual Jev) define how to keep typed, calibrated, cacheable answers.
9. Do not build a learned world model for Summer 1: the exact sim is a perfect world model. Build one later only for tracks without a sim (idea d), with Dreamer 4's recipe.
10. Do not run GRPO on Valen from raw sim reward (idea e). The sim gives exact labels; supervised distillation from search is strictly better signal per sample. Use RL only for the residual correction policy under latency.
11. Self-play against the previous ghost (idea b) is a curriculum trick, not a source of improvement; the planner's incumbent already is the ghost. Keep it as the N+1 bookkeeping.
12. Cheapest next step (under 2 h on this Mac): CEM/MPPI from mid-track snapshots on the robust objective, report finish time and finish rate at +/-2 ms jitter vs `opt/robust.mjs`.

Reading of the table:

- The racing winners separate perception from control and make control cheap. Linesight's whole net runs at 20 Hz on 160x120; Swift's policy is an MLP; MonoRace runs at 500 Hz. Nobody runs a 0.8B model per step. Our 86.7 ms Valen (`bench/infer/RESULTS.md`) is 1.7x Linesight's action period, so the model cannot be the per-ms controller, it must be the corrector (what `DESIGN.md` already says).
- Linesight's three non-obvious tricks: (1) "mini-races", Q is the undiscounted reward over the next 7 s, so gamma = 1 and the horizon is bounded (config `temporal_mini_race_duration_ms = 7000`); (2) reward per metre along a reference line plus a constant time penalty (`reward_per_m_advanced_along_centerline = 5/500`, `constant_reward_per_ms = -6/5000`); (3) 5% of transitions held out as a hidden test buffer to watch overfitting. Source: https://linesight-rl.github.io/linesight/build/html/main_objects.html
- The "last 0.1%" is selection everywhere: Linesight writes the best run's inputs to disk, Sophy kept intermediate policies and took 8 to 10 extra days for the final seconds. Records come from the max over many runs, not the mean. For a live, from-pixels attempt that is a problem: we get one shot per run, and the WR line has zero margin (0/40 finishes at +/-1 ms key jitter, `loop/score.mjs`). This is why robust search (idea c) is not optional.
- Sim-to-real worked when the gap was measured, not imagined: Swift fit residual perception and dynamics models from 3 real rollouts, MonoRace refined camera calibration from onboard logs. Our analogue is the measured key-timing distribution (CDP p50 0.1 ms, max 2.9 ms, `LOOP.md` row 4) and the capture-to-pose error of the vision model, both injected into the planner.

## 2. What is new in 2026 (and what is not)

- **RL fine-tuning of VLAs with verifiable rewards is now routine.** VLA-RL (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18719), SimpleVLA-RL (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09674) and the RL4VLA study (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19789) all report RL beats SFT on out-of-distribution execution. RSIPO (Oct 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15772) combines RLVR with on-policy self-distillation. Relevance: the machinery exists to RL-tune a small VLM on a binary "finished faster" reward. Our judgment: with an exact sim we have dense exact labels, so distillation dominates; keep RL for the residual.
- **Latency-aware RL is the 2026 result that maps directly onto our loop.** ARLI (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23831) shows standard RL fails under inference delay unless the state is augmented with the committed actions and a mid-inference observation; Real-Time EXPO-FT (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18207) keeps the big VLA as a slow proposer and trains a lightweight fast edit policy. That is our L0/L1 split: planner proposes, a tiny net edits, Valen corrects at 86 ms.
- **World models reached real-time, single-GPU, offline-only training.** Dreamer 4 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527). JEPA-style latent world models and GUI latent world-action models (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14228) are the October 2026 wave. Relevance: only for tracks without a sim (idea d). For Summer 1 the exact sim is the world model, and it is bit-exact, which no learned model will be.
- **Test-time training for agents** is active (Agentic-TTT https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12002, ReTTT https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23942, World-T3 https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17434). Relevance: our L1 "cerebellum" loop (latency estimate and gains updated within a run) is a TTT loop over a 3-parameter model; a LoRA TTT on Valen between runs is the N+1 loop. Nothing here changes the plan, it names it.
- **Self-improving agents gated by verifiers** (Terminal-RSI https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22192, recoverability and verifier-strength preconditions https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27465). Relevance: our verifier is the sim's Verify path and the game HUD; it is a strong verifier by that paper's definition, which is why an unattended N+1 loop is safe here.
- **System One decision models matured as a category.** Visual Jev (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25845): encode image and public context once, batch isolated question suffixes, 8.9x faster at N=32 questions, answer-supervised post-training lifts macro accuracy 70.6% to 76.1%, and a typed head gave no consistent gain over the LM-head readout. Jeva (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18773) formalizes null-aware, permutation-invariant, option-scalable, cacheable decisions. Narrow Decision Models (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25553) argue for small organization-specific decision models with a prefix-tree block mask so each answer is independent of the other questions. Relevance: our question-first streaming and the Box edge-distribution head are consistent with all three; the Visual Jev finding "adapt the backbone, keep the readout" matches `research/ENGINE.md` point 5 (head-only retrain tops out at 64%, backbone LoRA needed).
- **Fast computer-use agents** are mostly about token pruning and recurrent-state reuse on device: STP-GUI (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20390), Tandem on mobile NPUs saving hybrid linear-attention recurrent state where prompts diverge (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26660). Relevance: Tandem's checkpoint policy is exactly our DeltaNet prefix cache (21 MB memcpy restore, 32/32 agreement, `research/ENGINE.md`). Our general-computer-use story is the same engine with different questions.
- **Not new:** per-step VLM control at 100+ Hz. No 2026 paper runs a billion-parameter model inside a sub-10 ms loop; everyone decouples (slow proposer, fast editor).

## 3. Our unfair advantages and the strongest combination

What they lacked, checked in code:

- Linesight's `game_instance_manager.py` calls `rewind_to_state` only for the saved map start state and for the finish-prevention trick, never for mid-run planning (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/main/trackmania_rl/tmi_interaction/game_instance_manager.py). TMInterface does ship native save states and a bruteforce script (https://donadigo.com/tminterface/), but Linesight is pure model-free RL at ~10x realtime with rendering in the loop (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40853834).
- GT runs in real time only and cannot be rewound; Sophy's compute was 10 to 20 PS4s for 10+ days (https://ai.sony/blog/training-the-worlds-fastest-gran-turismo-racer).
- We have: byte-exact 1 ms physics at 114k ticks/s per core (114x realtime), 14 cores, whole-heap snapshot 23 MB in 0.6 ms and restore in 0.4 ms (`ctrl/sim.mjs`), the real game's Verify path as the oracle, and a headless render path for labelled frames. One core simulates a full 22 s run in ~0.2 s; 14 cores evaluate ~70 full runs per second, or ~250k candidate runs per hour.

Strongest combination (search for the line, learning for speed, VLM for perception):

1. **Line = search in the exact sim.** TAS-style toggle search already exists (`opt/search.mjs`, `opt/robust.mjs`). Add state-space search: from snapshots every 50 ms along the incumbent, run CEM or MPPI over the next 300 to 700 ms of toggles (Linesight's 7 s horizon is for a value function; a local planner with an exact model needs far less) with a terminal value. This is Go-Explore's "return then explore" with determinism (https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995) applied to a racing line.
2. **Value and policy nets to make search cheap.** A small net V(state) = predicted remaining time to finish, trained on the planner's own rollouts (exact labels), replaces long rollouts as the terminal value, AlphaZero-style (https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815). A policy prior over the 32 masks from the same data makes CEM sample where the planner would. Both are state-input, tiny, CPU-fast, and never see human data.
3. **Robustness as the objective, not an afterthought.** Optimize expected finish time under the measured jitter and latency distribution (the `loop/score.mjs` model), with common random numbers (already in `opt/robust.mjs`). The planner's output is a line plus a tube of states from which a known correction recovers.
4. **Perception = System One questions, trained from search-labelled frames.** Every frame of every planner rollout has an exact pose, hitbox and the planner's correction. Valen answers car Box, off_line noul, steer and throttle choice; a ViT-S student (ENGINE.md lever 4) takes over when its confidence clears the gate.
5. **Runtime = feedforward line + fast residual + slow corrector**, exactly the ARLI / Real-Time EXPO-FT split (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23831 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18207): 1 ms keys from the plan, an 8 ms residual from the controller (`ctrl/controller.mjs`), an 86 ms (later ~30 ms) correction from Valen when the car box disagrees with the predicted box.

## 4. Three novel ideas (plus the candidates, scored)

**A. Search-distilled System One with jitter-robust expert iteration (candidates a + c merged).**
Mechanism: expert iteration where the expert is CEM/MPPI in the exact sim from snapshots under injected jitter, and the student is Valen with typed answers. For each snapshot state s and each jitter sample, the expert computes the best next 8 ms mask sequence; the label for the frame rendered at s is (steer choice, throttle choice, car box, off_line noul) plus the expert's margin. Train LoRA on the backbone, question-first order, Visual Jev style (adapt backbone, keep readout, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25845). Iterate: the student's live runs become new snapshot seeds (N+1).
Why here: AlphaZero's gain came from search being a better teacher than reward (https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815); our search is exact and 114x realtime, and the student does not need to plan, only to recognise which basin it is in. Robustness is baked in because the labels come from the jittered expert, so the live policy inherits the margin.
Experiment (< 2 h): take the incumbent line (`opt/robust.json`), snapshot every 50 ms (440 states), from each run CEM with 64 samples x 500 ms horizon x +/-2 ms jitter on the `loop/score.mjs` cost, using 14 workers. Report: mean margin improvement per state and the finish rate of the re-stitched line vs the incumbent (node, no GPU). Success = finish rate under +/-2 ms jitter rises from the incumbent's value to >= 0.9 without losing more than 20 ms of finish time.
P(success) 0.65. Most likely failure: 500 ms local horizons miss setup moves for jumps (need 1.5 to 3 s on the two airborne sections, visible in `opt/_probe_jump.mjs`).

**B. Margin-aware line: optimise the funnel, not the point.**
Mechanism: replace "finish time of one trajectory" with "finish time of the worst 5% of a tube": for each toggle, sample delivery offsets from the measured CDP key distribution and the controller's observed pose error, and score the line by the 5th-percentile finish time with the closed-loop controller in the loop (`ctrl/episode.mjs` already simulates latency, jitter, noise). Then let the search spend ms of lap time to buy ms of margin where the sensitivity map (`opt/sensitivity.mjs`) says the line is knife-edged.
Why here: the WR is a point solution with 0/40 jitter finishes. GT Sophy's distributional critic (QR-SAC, 32 quantiles) existed precisely to drive at the limit under outcome uncertainty (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09021 sec. III-D); we can compute that quantile exactly instead of learning it. Nobody in the Trackmania lineage optimises the quantile because they could not re-simulate.
Experiment (< 2 h): run `opt/robust.mjs` with the cost changed to the 5th-percentile closed-loop finish over 32 episode samples (latency 60 ms, jitter 2 ms, noise 0.05 m) for 30 min on 12 workers; report p05/p50 finish and finish rate vs the current incumbent under the same sampler.
P(success) 0.75. Most likely failure: the closed-loop controller, not the line, is the bottleneck (then the fix is controller gains, which the same harness measures).

**C. Pose-from-pixels as a Box question, with the sim as the only labeller, and a confidence-gated student (new).**
Mechanism: the live loop needs car pose, not steering, from the frame. Train the Box head (4 edge distributions on the 12x22 pre-merge ViT grid, `research/ENGINE.md` point 6) on headless-rendered frames from the planner's rollouts, where the label is the exact projected hitbox. Then train a ViT-S/16 student on the same labels with Valen as the fallback, gated by the Box entropy. The controller consumes the box centre and width as (lateral offset, distance), which is what `ctrl/controller.mjs` needs.
Why here: Swift and MonoRace won with a gate detector plus a Kalman filter, not with end-to-end pixels-to-motor (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06419-4 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15222). The vision GT7 agent needed a privileged critic to learn from 64x64 pixels (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09021). We have the privileged state for free and a typed Box answer with calibrated confidence, so the perception problem collapses to supervised regression with unlimited exact labels.
Experiment (< 2 h): render 2k frames at 256x144 from 2k planner snapshots (headless Metal path, `LOOP.md` row 0), fit the Box head with a frozen backbone on 1.6k, report mean IoU and edge error in px on 400 held out; then measure the controller's finish rate in `ctrl/episode.mjs` with observation noise set to the measured edge error instead of 0.05 m.
P(success) 0.7 for IoU >= 0.7 with a frozen backbone; most likely failure: the 2x2 merge hides a 20 px car (then tap the pre-merge grid as ENGINE.md says).

Candidate scoring (a to f from the brief):
- (a) search-distilled System One: core of A. Keep.
- (b) ghost self-play: the planner incumbent is the ghost; add "beat the ghost by >= 1 ms at every checkpoint" as a curriculum gate for N+1. Cheap, low upside, keep as bookkeeping. P 0.9 it works, P 0.1 it matters.
- (c) robust-line search: core of B. Keep, highest P.
- (d) pixel world model for tracks without a sim: Dreamer 4 recipe (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527) on the headless renderer's video plus keys; needed only for generalization claims. Defer. P 0.4 of beating a sim-less baseline on a new track in a week.
- (e) GRPO on Valen's typed decisions with sim reward: rejected as the main signal (exact labels exist). Allowed later for the residual edit policy under latency, following ARLI (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23831). P 0.3 that it beats distillation on this track.
- (f) 14-track curriculum: do it for perception (Box, off_line) since those questions are track-agnostic, not for control. The vision GT7 agent's regularizers (random shift, reinit after buffer fill, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09021 sec. III-E) are the ones to copy. P 0.6 that held-out-track Box IoU stays within 5 points of the training tracks.

Delete list (first-principles pass): no GRPO on the backbone, no learned world model for Summer 1, no 7 s value horizon (exact search with a learned terminal value is shorter and exact), no per-ms VLM control, no human ghost anywhere.
