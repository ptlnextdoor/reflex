## 1. What Jev's vision does, and where it is weak

[S] Jev 1.14 (2026-10-14, hosted only, `jev-preview`): PNG/JPEG images listed once in a top-level `assets` map and referenced anywhere text goes, state, instructions or criteria, so a Choice option, Score level or Noul true/false can be defined by example pictures; frame histories in state; a Box question type returning top/bottom/left/right as fractions plus a confidence, one box per question; documented patterns: compare against a reference picture, what changed across frames, look closer by re-asking on a crop, tile search via a Choice over tiles, edge-check Nouls (caught 30 of 36 cut-off crops). Limits: each side 280 to 1568 px, 78,400 to 2.46 M pixels, 16 MiB, rejected not resized; all behind `TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images: true`; "dreamer-ready", not production; TypeSafe says all models do worse on images than text. https://docs.typesafe.ai/images , https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/box , https://typesafe.ai/blog/jev-1-14
Weak spots: (a) [S] network round trip; TypeSafe publishes no latency, launch coverage said ~150 ms, a builder reported ~200 ms for a 4-frame request (https://madewithjev.com/jev-images); (b) [S] 280 px minimum side, so a 256x144 game frame is rejected; (c) [S] Box has no not-found and must be paired with a Noul; (d) [S] no published Box precision, no multi-box, no customer fine-tuning or LoRA (https://docs.typesafe.ai/models); (e) [S] each reference re-adds the image's tokens, no cross-request reuse of an encoded frame; (f) [S] jaggedness: literal reading, weak numbers, "do not use score expectation for magnitude" (https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13).

## 2. Laya + same + more

Keep Laya's recipe, not Laya's weights. Laya's value is the typed head read at `[MASK]` option markers on a bidirectional encoder (no option-order bias), the RLCD proper-scoring loss, temperature calibration, Apache-2.0 and the `/v1/systemone` contract. Its backbone, text-only ModernBERT-large, is the wrong starting point for vision: [S] the laya-vision fork tried exactly "a SigLIP projector into Laya's text encoder" and it never learned to use the image in 5 runs (shuffled images scored the same as real ones), while the same head on ModernVBERT, a ModernBERT-150M already MLM-aligned to SigLIP2-B/16 for 10B tokens (MIT, in transformers >= 5.3), was their best vision backbone, 65.2% A-OKVQA, 250M params, 32 ms on an L4 (https://r33drichards.github.io/laya-vision/reference/results/what-didnt-work/ , https://r33drichards.github.io/laya-vision/concepts/architecture/ , https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01149). [M] It is also 2.9x cheaper per token on this GPU (7.1 vs 20.3 ms at 128 tokens). DESIGN.md's P 0.6 for Laya-large plus a fresh projector reaching IoU 0.7 is, on this evidence, nearer 0.3 [E].

Architecture, "Laya-V": SigLIP2-B/16 run at native 256x144 (144 patches, no resample, positions interpolated; the student branch already proves 256x144 is enough for a 0.77 IoU box) [M]; 2x2 shuffle to 36 visual tokens; ModernVBERT text tower, bidirectional; Laya's head (type embedding, 2 head layers, scorer at markers, act head); all N questions packed in one row with a block mask so each question's markers see the image and state but not other questions (Jeva/NDM-style isolation, trained that way from day one; sys1rust REPORT item 5: one encoder pass for all questions is the single biggest win, and packing needs a model trained for it) [S]. Answer types: choice, score, noul as Laya; Box as four edge distributions over the 16 columns and 9 rows of the ViT grid with DFL loss, expectation for sub-bin edges, entropy for confidence (ENGINE.md item 6, moved to the native grid); plus `present`, a Noul fused into the Box answer, which is the not-found Jev lacks. "More" that Jev does not have: metric Score (ordered levels trained on exact sim quantities so the expectation is a calibrated measurement in metres or radians, the thing Jev's docs say not to do); null-aware Box; two-frame state at 36 tokens per frame; 144 px inputs; fixed-shape zero-allocation streaming (see 3); local, fine-tunable, open weights.

Data and recipe (stage C fits a few hours on this Mac on AC, 250M params and 5k frames; stage A is one cloud GPU-day): stage A, Laya head plus LoRA on the top ModernVBERT layers on permissive Cauldron subsets (A-OKVQA, VQAv2 yes/no, no ScienceQA, which drags in CC BY-NC-SA) for general visual choice/noul/score parity; stage B, Box head on sim hitboxes and next-gate boxes plus a permissive referring-box set; stage C, Polytrack: sim-labeled frames (data engine, 17 frames/s measured [M]) with lateral offset, heading error, airborne, on_road, next-gate box and the planner's steer/throttle. Calibrate temperatures on held-out tracks only (laya-vision found fitting on the trained game's own episodes over-flattens) [S]. Hold out winter1 as the student does. Valen is not needed as a teacher: the sim gives exact labels and the Cauldron gives general ones.

## 3. Latency plan (M4 Pro)

Budget per frame, 1 frame, 5 packed questions, ~36 visual + ~110 text tokens: ViT 6.7 ms [M], text tower 1x160 ~10 ms [M], head and host ~1.5 ms [E], **~18 ms p50 in Python MLX, ~14 ms in Rust** after sys1rust's exact levers (compiled fixed-shape graph padded to 16, dense attention under 1k tokens, unpad, head pruned to marker rows, fused qkv/RoPE Metal kernel; together 1.25x over Python on an M5 with no answer change) [S sys1rust/results/SPEED.md]. Not carried over: NAX gemms (M5 only), int8 (no MLX int8 matmul, failed the 99% gate) [S]. Nothing in a bidirectional encoder is cacheable across frames (every key depends on the image) [S laya-vision game-caching], so the streaming win is fixed shapes, not KV reuse; laya-vision got 3.4 to 5x at batch 1 from exactly that on CUDA [S]. p99 under 2x p50 with fixed shapes [S sys1rust]. 120 Hz (8.3 ms) is for the ViT-Ti student (2.2 to 4.3 ms median, 1.5 ms floor [M track/student]) answering the four driving Scores/Nouls; Laya-V runs at ~60 Hz as arbiter and Box. Honest comparison: Jev's round trip is ~150 ms (launch coverage, https://elsolitario.org/en/2026/09/19/laya-convai-decision-engine-33ms/) to ~200 ms (builder report above) with no official figure, so local 14 to 18 ms is ~10x on latency, while Jev is almost surely larger and better on open-world images; we beat it on latency, resolution floor, null-aware Box and metric Score, not on general accuracy. P(Laya-V p50 <= 20 ms at 5 questions on this Mac) 0.8; most likely miss: SigLIP2's fixed 32x32 position grid interpolated to 16x9 costs accuracy; fallback is a 256x256 letterbox (256 patches, 64 tokens, ViT ~12 ms, e2e ~25 ms [E]), not 512 px tiles.

## 4. Polytrack under 21.0 s

Arithmetic: 1,293 m in 21.0 s needs 221 km/h average against today's 176 [M]. Segment averages today: start 124, cp1 to cp2 161, cp2 to cp3 190, cp3 to finish 207 km/h [M]. Time lost against a 220 km/h pace: cp1 to cp2 2.3 s (4 s flight, landing at 99 km/h, 91 to 137 km/h recovery), cp2 to cp3 0.8 s, cp3 to finish 0.5 s (second flight); the 1.8 s on the standing start is mostly inherent (0 to 216 km/h takes 3.5 s) [E]. Fixing the three segments gives ~22.9 s; the last 1.9 s must come from a shorter path or a pace above 220 (every 60 m, or 10 km/h of average, is ~1 s) [E]. So the line is a search problem on two jumps plus corner cutting, and the current planner cannot see it: a 2.5 s greedy horizon commits into a 4 s flight, and the owner's 6 s-horizon run finished at 33.1 s [M learn/plan_h6.log], consistent with CEM over 120 fine segments collapsing [E]. Fixes, in order: hierarchical CEM (200 ms segments over 6 s, 50 ms over the first second) from snapshots every 50 ms along the incumbent; a tiny V(state) = remaining time trained on the planner's own rollouts as terminal value (AlphaZero-style, SOTA step 3); Go-Explore cells (checkpoint, speed bucket, lateral bucket) so jump approaches get restarted rather than refined (refine.mjs gained 180 ms in 300 rounds [M], it is plateaued); then the p05-under-jitter closed-loop objective (0/20 finishes at 1 ms today [M]). P(sim lap < 21.0 s in two weeks of CPU) 0.35 to 0.45, P(< 22.123) 0.65; most likely failure: the physics ceiling on this path, which the cp1 probe in section 6 exposes first.

Live from pixels, who does what: the planner (offline) owns the 1 ms toggle schedule and a tube of expected poses plus recovery branches from snapshots; the controller (`ctrl/controller.mjs`, 8 ms, CPU, 35/36 grid cells at 60 ms latency and 0.05 m noise [M LOOP row 11]) owns steering and throttle residuals from {lateral offset, heading error, speed}; the model's only job per frame is measuring those. Here the current plan is wrong: it drives the controller from the car's Box, but in a chase cam the car barely moves on screen (centre sd 12 to 15 px, constant-box IoU 0.647 [M]), so the car box carries almost no pose. The per-frame questions become: Score lateral offset (9 levels over +/-2 m), Score heading error, Noul airborne, Noul on_road, Box next gate (it does move and encodes distance), Choice phase. Spec from ctrl: latency <= 120 ms, 0.2 m RMS, 0.25 m bias [M]; 0.2 m is several px of road at the car's depth in a 512-wide frame, resolvable [E]. Every live run is re-simulated from its sent keys for exact labels (data engine, error 0.0 [M]) and feeds N+1.
